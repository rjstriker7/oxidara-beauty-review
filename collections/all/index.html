Ingredient literacy

A few familiar names.
A little more understanding.

Learn what common cosmetic ingredients can do in a formula. These educational examples are not Oxidara ingredient lists and do not mean any product contains them.

Illustrative preview content. Ingredients and directions are not confirmed for this product.

01Hydration

Example ingredient profile

Hyaluronic acid

A humectant: an ingredient that binds water. In skincare, hyaluronic acid is used to help the surface of skin feel hydrated and supple. Its role depends on the complete formula.

02Conditioning

Example ingredient profile

Squalane

An emollient used to soften and smooth the feel of skin. This clear oil-like ingredient appears in many cosmetic textures; its presence alone does not determine a finished product’s richness or finish.

03Barrier support

Example ingredient profile

Ceramides

A family of lipids also found in the skin’s outer layer. Cosmetic ceramides, such as ceramide NP, are used in formulas designed around moisture retention and skin conditioning.

Your approach to application

Choose with intention.
Keep it personal.

The collection is an invitation to explore. It is not a requirement to use every product together, and no layering order is confirmed.

  1. 01

    Get to know the format

    Use the product overview to understand its place in the collection. An oil and a serum can look similar in a bottle while needing different guidance.

  2. 02

    Read the application preview

    Each beauty page includes a three-step example. These drafts show the intended clarity of the final guidance and remain subject to product-specific approval.

  3. 03

    Follow the final label

    Once available, check ingredients, suitability, directions and precautions together. Build a routine around your preferences and approved product information.
